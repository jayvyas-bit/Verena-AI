# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Small and mid-sized business owners and solo operators who personally handle
their own compliance — not compliance professionals, not developers, not the
person who maintains the website day to day. They are anxious but avoidant
(aware something could be wrong, not inclined to go looking for it), time-poor
(compliance is a side task on top of running the business), and skeptical of
"instant compliance" silver-bullet marketing (cookie banners, overlay widgets)
they've seen before. They are specifically wary of unsupervised AI making
legal judgment calls. They want delegation, not education: the win condition
is "someone trustworthy handled it," not "I now understand privacy law."

## Product Purpose

Verena is an AI agent platform that scans a website against privacy,
accessibility, security, SEO, and compliance gaps, surfaces findings with
supporting evidence, and proposes human-reviewed fixes (developer-ready code,
draft document revisions). A human (via Invictus Counsel) backs up anything
needing legal judgment — "AI does the first 90%, a human backs the last 10%,"
not "AI does your compliance" unsupervised. Success is the visitor trusting
the scan enough to run it on their own site.

## Brand Personality

Calm authority, not alarm. Grounded, confident, reassuring — a serious tool
for something consequential, not an enterprise-stiff legal product and not a
flashy AI-startup pitch. Taste references: Linear (restrained typographic
minimalism, careful motion easing), Arc (soft gradients, editorial copy voice,
playful without juvenile), Stripe (technical credibility blended with
warmth — the same "trust me with something sensitive" problem fintech
onboarding solves for money).

## Anti-references

- Generic "legal SaaS": boring, form-heavy, blue-and-grey.
- Generic "AI startup" cliché: gradient-on-black, hero-metric template,
  over-applied glassmorphism/bento-grid treated as automatically "modern."
- Fear-based compliance marketing: alarm red, warning iconography, urgency
  copy. The product's job is to read as calm authority, not scare the visitor
  into action.
- Flashy, trust-undermining AI marketing generally — this audience has
  specifically named skepticism of unsupervised-AI flashiness as a trust gap.

## Design Principles

1. Calm authority over alarm — grounded maroon reads as serious, not warning;
   no fear-based urgency copy or red alert iconography.
2. Human-in-the-loop is a trust feature — surface the human-backstop signal
   early and visibly, not as footnote caveat text.
3. One deliberate accent, used sparingly — #FCF56F carries emphasis, not
   decoration; it does not appear everywhere at once.
4. Motion restrained and purposeful — entrance and hover only, always gated
   behind `prefers-reduced-motion`.
5. Polish signals competence for this audience — a visitor deciding whether
   to trust an AI agent with legal exposure reads production-grade craft as
   evidence the product itself is well-built, not as sales theater.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA minimum — accessibility is Verena's own product category, so the
marketing site holding itself to that bar is part of the credibility story.
Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` throughout. Maintain strong contrast against
the cream/maroon/yellow palette; never rely on color alone to convey state.

# Confidence and gaps

Written in response to being asked "are you sure about this plan?" — this is
the honest calibration pass, kept as its own document rather than buried in
prose, so it doesn't get lost on a re-read later.

## High confidence (verified directly or via consistent multi-source data)
- Verena's own current site: copy, nav, pricing, tone (`00-verena-current-state.md`)
  — fetched directly from verena.ai.
- Directional shape of small-business compliance anxiety: LegalZoom's
  82%/33%/25% figures and BizFilings' 31% figure are real, published surveys
  and are directionally consistent with each other.
- The fragmentation angle (point tools vs. one Verena scan) — follows
  directly from Verena's own stated scope plus the existence of multiple
  single-category competitors; doesn't depend on any shaky pricing number.

## Reasonable inference — treat as hypothesis, not fact
- The "anxious-avoidant, AI-skeptical" audience emotional profile
  (`01-audience-emotion.md`) is this document's synthesis across the survey
  data and Verena's own caveat-heavy copy. Nobody was surveyed on "do you
  trust AI with your compliance." Validate against real beta-user feedback
  before treating it as settled.
- The Tier 1 / Tier 2 competitor split (`04-competitors.md`) is a structural
  read of the pricing/category data, not something any source stated
  outright.

## Weak — flagged explicitly, don't repeat as fact elsewhere
- **Competitor pricing** (Termly, CookieYes, Osano, accessiBe, UserWay) came
  mostly from affiliate/competitor blogs with numbers that **conflict for
  the same product** (e.g., Termly quoted at both ~$15/mo and $180/yr by
  different sources). Re-verify on each vendor's own pricing page before
  using these in any real pricing or positioning decision.
- **The accessiBe/FTC scrutiny claim** — the search tool itself said it could
  not confirm this against a primary source. It was included in the original
  research answer with more confidence than it deserved. Needs independent
  legal verification before being cited anywhere external.
- **Drata/Sprinto/Scrut funding figures** — no verified data found; none
  should appear as specifics anywhere downstream of this research.
- **Ramp/Mercury as design benchmarks** — no usable source material returned;
  the mention in `02-tech-company-benchmarks.md` is a placeholder for a gap,
  not a finding.
- **Linear/Arc/Stripe as benchmarks** — these are design judgment applied to
  the brief, using general prior knowledge, not a fresh sourced pull of their
  current brand pages in this research pass. Fine as taste reference, not as
  a "the research shows" claim.
- **The Forrester "AI Agents" GRC criterion** (`03-global-startups.md`) — a
  single contextual mention, not confirmed against a primary Forrester
  report.

## Bias note on the primary audience-emotion sources
LegalZoom and BizFilings/Wolters Kluwer both sell compliance services to the
same small-business audience being surveyed. Their surveys have a commercial
interest in finding compliance to be a painful, widespread problem. The
direction of their findings is still plausible (and corroborated across two
independently-run surveys), but the exact percentages shouldn't be treated as
neutral, bias-free data.

## Bottom line
Act on: the audience-emotion framing (as hypothesis) and the fragmentation
differentiation angle — both are reasonably well-grounded. Don't act on:
specific competitor prices, the FTC claim, or startup funding figures without
independent re-verification first.
